# Store-copy evidence

Sources checked August 12, 2026.

## Official guidance

- [Google Play: Best practices for your store listing](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13393723)
  says descriptions should accurately describe the product, explain what makes it distinctive, and
  stay succinct because people may read only the first few sentences.
- [Google Play: Helpful tips to get published](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15191715)
  says the listing should explain what the product does, why it is valuable, and what users should
  expect. The shipping product must deliver every promise.
- [Google Play: Store listing experiments](https://play.google.com/console/about/store-listing-experiments/)
  provides controlled experiments for copy and creative changes. Treat conversion as a testable
  result, not something a writer can guarantee.
- [Apple: Creating your product page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)
  recommends a concise, informative paragraph followed by a short list of main features.

## Research interpretation

Competitor listings reveal category vocabulary, expected capabilities, common objections, and tired
claims. They do not prove what wording will convert or what the product being marketed can do.

Reviews reveal the outcomes people value and the friction they notice after installation. Sample
both positive and negative reviews, distinguish recurring themes from isolated opinions, and verify
every resulting claim against the shipping product.

Store experiments provide stronger conversion evidence than taste or precedent. Change one major
hypothesis at a time, run the experiment long enough to reduce noise, and keep policy compliance and
claim accuracy fixed across variants.
